Layering to MIDI

Layer an audio track onto MIDI tracks with TRACK WILL TRIGGER. The MIDI tracks then play along over MIDI: every note, chord and release that your audio track plays. Select your firmware file. Then download a signed image.

Make sure that you can go back to stock before you flash a modified file. The Early Start-up Menu flashes the stock firmware over MIDI. Do this one time with the stock file, before you flash a modified file. Back up your +Drive. No tool here can recover it.

What changes

Set up
On the audio track, TRACK WILL TRIGGER: select one or more MIDI tracks
Playback
Every note that the audio track plays (trigs, chords, retrigs, probability, held keys) also goes out on the channel of each layered MIDI track
Releases
Note-off when the layered note ends: at its length, when a held key is let go, or at stop for INF
Combining
Several audio tracks can layer onto one MIDI track, and one audio track onto several
Mutes
A muted source sends nothing. A muted MIDI track sends nothing
Not layered
Every track behaves as stock
Writes
—

Confirmed on a Digitone II, 2026-10-02. We recorded into Ableton from an external synth on the layered MIDI track. Sequenced notes, chords, retrigs and probability arrive as played. INF holds until the sequencer stops. Held keys release cleanly. Two sources into one MIDI track work, and one source into several MIDI tracks works.

The MIDI note arrived about 8 ms before the recorded audio. That delay is the latency of the audio path, not of the mod.

3

Build it

The image is rebuilt, re-signed with the key derived from your own file, and every integrity field is checked again. Nothing downloads unless all of them pass.

Not with the MIDI Arpeggiator

MIDI Arpeggiator also turns layered notes on MIDI tracks into MIDI, when the MIDI track's own arp is on. The two no longer share any bytes, but until that overlap is checked they are kept apart: they cannot be built together.
